How the dividend mechanism works

🏦 Strategy raises cash by issuing STRC shares
→
₿ They use that cash to buy more Bitcoin
→
💸 Their BTC holdings generate value to fund your payments
→
📬 You receive cash twice a month

The rate is variable — Strategy adjusts it each month with one goal: keep the share price close to $100. If the price drops below $100, they raise the dividend to attract buyers. If it climbs above, they can lower it. Volatility shows up in the yield, not the price. That said, the price has still drifted below $100 recently as market conditions tightened.

⚠️

How long can they pay — even if Bitcoin struggles?

Strategy currently holds roughly $871M in cash reserves — enough to cover approximately 6 months of STRC dividends without selling a single Bitcoin. If needed, they can also sell BTC (they recently did for the first time in their history). Longer term, the model depends on Bitcoin holding value and on continued investor demand for their preferred stock offerings. This is a real risk to understand: it's not a guaranteed-forever payment.

💡
The tax angle most people miss: STRC dividends are classified as Return of Capital by the IRS — meaning you do not pay income tax when you receive them. Instead, each payment quietly reduces your cost basis. You only pay tax (at low long-term capital gains rates) when you eventually sell. Strategy expects this treatment to last 10+ years. Tax-deferred compounding is the next best thing to tax-free.
Why it's appealing
  • Income that doesn't require Bitcoin to go up
  • Dividends are tax-deferred (Return of Capital)
  • Trading below $100 par — you're buying at a discount
  • Semi-monthly payments starting now
  • Rate raised 7 times since launch
Risks to know
  • Cash reserves cover ~6 months — not unlimited
  • Price has dropped to ~$88 — below the $100 target
  • Strategy broke its "never sell Bitcoin" rule in 2026
  • Dividend can be cut if Strategy faces pressure
  • Not FDIC insured
Bottom line: High yield with a real tax advantage — but you're betting on Strategy's financial health and Bitcoin's long-term value. Best suited for someone comfortable holding through short-term price swings and who doesn't need to sell immediately.

How the dividend mechanism works

💼 Strive holds Bitcoin + cash as assets
→
📊 Those assets generate returns and appreciation
→
🔄 Board sets rate monthly to keep price near $100
→
📬 You receive cash every trading day

The stated rate is 13% per year. Because payments arrive every single trading day (roughly 250 days/year), that daily compounding bumps the effective yield to ~13.88%. Strive can raise or lower the rate monthly to keep the share price inside their $95–$105 target range. If the price dips, they raise the rate. If it climbs, they may lower it slightly.

🛡️

How long can they pay — even if Bitcoin struggles?

Strive holds 15,009 BTC plus 18 months of cash reserves set aside specifically for dividend payments. Daily dividend obligations run ~$390,000/day — covered roughly 21 times over by current assets. Their CEO has publicly stated the balance sheet can survive a 2022-style Bitcoin crash (when BTC dropped ~75%) without cutting dividends. The company carries no debt senior to SATA, which means you're first in line. Independent analysts estimate they can sustain payments for approximately 20 years at current levels.

Why it's appealing
  • Highest yield of the two options at ~13.88%
  • Income literally every trading day
  • 1.9× asset coverage — real backing
  • 20-year estimated coverage at current rates
  • Debt-free — you're first in line
  • Slight discount today (~$97.71)
Risks to know
  • BTC crash erodes the asset cushion
  • Board can reduce dividend rate unilaterally
  • Below $100 now — selling quickly could be a small loss
  • Strive is newer and smaller than Strategy
  • Ordinary income tax (not Return of Capital)
Bottom line: The stronger sustainability story of the two, with more backing and a longer runway. The daily compounding and 20-year coverage estimate make this feel like the more durable bet — at the cost of a slightly higher tax bill each year.
